# Candidate C — Travel and Activity Discovery

**Geography considered:** Pattaya  
**Research snapshot:** 2026-11-24  
**Evidence state:** desk research; no traveller or supplier conversations

## Candidate problem space

A possible concept is to help travellers compare fragmented local activities and complete reliable bookings across multiple suppliers.

This is not yet a demonstrated customer problem. The existence of many activities or suppliers does not itself establish that travellers cannot discover, compare, trust, or book them adequately.

## Incumbent distribution

### Klook

Klook's Pattaya destination page displayed **152 results** when accessed for this research snapshot. The catalogue includes a mix of attractions, shows, water activities, tours, and other experiences, with examples showing instant confirmation, free cancellation, ratings, and substantial booking counts.

Sources:
- [Klook — Best Things to Do in Pattaya](https://www.klook.com/destination/c17-pattaya/1-things-to-do/)
- [Klook — Pattaya destination](https://www.klook.com/destination/c17-pattaya/)

The result count and catalogue are dynamic and may change by locale, page, availability, and date. The count is a snapshot, not a permanent market-size figure. Klook's displayed ratings and booking figures are platform-provided signals, not independent verification.

### GetYourGuide: supplier vs reseller routes

GetYourGuide's supplier guidance says it works with registered businesses and individual traders that directly operate their own tours, activities, or attractions. It says it does not accept resellers, aggregators, OTAs, DMCs, or unregistered private guides as **supply partners**.

Source: [Who can apply to be a Supply Partner?](https://supply.getyourguide.support/hc/en-us/articles/13980993520925-Who-can-apply-to-be-a-Supply-Partner) (updated 24 March 2026).

This does **not** mean reselling or affiliate distribution is impossible. GetYourGuide separately documents supplier affiliate and cross-selling routes through which eligible partners can recommend other activities and earn commission.

Sources:
- [Supplier affiliate program: Intro](https://partner.getyourguide.support/hc/en-us/articles/22154259422237-Supplier-affiliate-program-Intro)
- [Cross-selling for activity providers](https://partner.getyourguide.support/hc/en-us/articles/13981102130973-Cross-selling-for-activity-providers)

The distinction matters: direct supply onboarding and referral/reseller partnerships are different business models with different eligibility, control, economics, and customer responsibilities.

## Why a new broad aggregator is unattractive on current evidence

- A large incumbent already offers a broad Pattaya activity catalogue and mature booking features.
- Distribution and supplier relationships are likely to be central to any competing marketplace.
- A $0, no-cold-outreach, no-public-publishing constraint makes it difficult to build both trusted supply and consumer demand.
- An affiliate/reseller route may be available, but that alone does not create differentiated customer value or a defensible business.
- No consumer problem survived this desk-research pass with enough evidence to justify a new intermediary.

## Critical unknowns

- Which traveller segment is underserved by current platforms?
- What recent booking decision failed or created meaningful friction?
- Is the gap in discovery, trust, language, availability, payment, transport, support, or something else?
- Does a specific supplier segment want incremental distribution, and on what economics?
- What is the cost of acquiring travellers and maintaining accurate inventory?
- Can the concept deliver value without owning fulfilment, refunds, safety, or supplier quality?

## Assessment

**Stop the broad aggregator / new intermediary direction on current evidence.** Do not build a catalogue or concierge transaction flow merely because local activities are fragmented. Reopen only if a specific traveller or supplier segment reveals a repeated, consequential problem that incumbents do not solve and that can be reached economically.
