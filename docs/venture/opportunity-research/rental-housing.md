# Candidate B — Rental Housing

**Geography considered:** Thailand, with Pattaya as a possible initial market  
**Research snapshot:** 2026-11-24  
**Evidence state:** desk research; no renter, landlord, or agent conversations

## Candidate problem space

Potential renter-side problems include stale or duplicated listings, unclear availability, misleading photos/details, scams, fragmented communication, and uncertainty about total move-in cost.

Potential landlord/agent-side problems include lead quality, verification, listing distribution, tenant screening, coordination, and compliance administration.

These are plausible problem categories, not established local pain findings. This review did not find sufficient independent evidence to claim that a particular one is frequent, costly, underserved, or commercially attractive in Pattaya.

## Existing alternatives

### Property portals and agent services

DDproperty / PropertyGuru offers agent verification and certification. Its published certification process requires identity and contact verification, and agent certification can depend on verifiable transactions or recognized training. PropertyGuru also publishes paid annual agent packages, with displayed prices ranging from THB 10,250 to THB 260,130.

Sources:
- [DDproperty Agent Certification](https://www.agentofferings.ddproperty.com/certification/?lang=en)
- [DDproperty agent packages](https://www.agentofferings.ddproperty.com/upgrade/)

This demonstrates an established paid agent-services market and existing trust features. It does not prove that verification is effective in practice or that renters' trust problems are solved.

### Brokerage and rental support

PropertyScout describes a tenant-facing service that arranges viewings, provides a standard contract, and supports tenants during the lease. It states that tenants pay no service fee and that the landlord typically pays a success-based commission of one month's rent. Its landlord page similarly describes no upfront cost and a success-based commission.

Sources:
- [PropertyScout — How it works](https://propertyscout.co.th/en/how-does-it-work/)
- [PropertyScout — List your property](https://propertyscout.co.th/en/list-your-property/)

These are company-published claims and descriptions of its own service model.

## Compliance lead — not a product conclusion

A Fragomen alert dated 7 May 2025 states that Thailand's Board of Investment requires a TM30 residence notification as supporting evidence for initial and renewal long-stay visa applications filed through the BOI Single Window System. It also describes the filing responsibility as belonging to the property owner, landlord, or accommodation owner.

Source: [Thailand: Residence Notification Now Required for Certain Visa Applications](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/thailand-residence-notification-now-required-for-certain-visa-applications.html) (Fragomen, 7 May 2025).

**Scope warning:** this source concerns certain BOI Single Window long-stay visa applications. It must not be generalized into a claim that TM30 proof is required for every Thai visa application or every rental transaction.

A landlord-side compliance workflow could be explored separately, but the alert alone does not establish:
- that landlords experience a recurring administrative problem;
- that existing property managers or agents fail to handle it;
- that a new software product is needed;
- that the founder can legally provide the proposed service.

Any pilot involving property, immigration, agency, or document handling requires primary-source legal review. Law-firm or vendor summaries are not a substitute for advice on the specific operating model.

## Why a generic discovery portal is not supported

The current evidence shows established portals, agent verification, viewing coordination, and success-based brokerage. It does not demonstrate a specific information gap that a new portal could fill better, nor a viable route to acquire both trustworthy supply and renter demand at $0 budget.

A new listing marketplace would also face cold-start, freshness, verification, fraud, and customer-support burdens. No evidence currently shows a defensible advantage over incumbents.

## Critical unknowns

- Which exact renter or landlord segment experiences the most consequential problem?
- How often do stale, duplicate, or misleading listings cause wasted time or money?
- Who verifies availability and property authority today?
- What does a failed viewing or failed rental transaction cost?
- Which party pays for the current service and what outcome do they buy?
- What do portals and local agents already solve adequately?
- What regulatory, licensing, foreign-business, work-permit, privacy, and consumer-protection constraints apply to the proposed model?

## Assessment

**Do not pursue a generic rental-listing portal on current evidence.** Landlord-side compliance is a secondary research lead only. It should not be promoted to a product opportunity until a real, repeated, costly workflow problem and a lawful delivery model are demonstrated.
