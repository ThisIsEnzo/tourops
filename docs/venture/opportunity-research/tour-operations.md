# Candidate A — Tour Operations

**Geography considered:** Pattaya, Na Jomtien, Ocean Marina, Sattahip, Rayong  
**Research snapshot:** 2026-11-24  
**Evidence state:** desk research; no operator conversations or workflow observation

## Candidate problem space

Small marine-tour and excursion operators may coordinate enquiries, quotes, deposits, passengers, crews, vessels, equipment, weather decisions, and departure readiness across a mixture of chat, spreadsheets, paper, and booking platforms.

That description is a **hypothesis**, not an observed account of how local operators work. No reliable public source in this review establishes the actual tool stack or frequency/cost of coordination failures among small operators in the target area.

## What public evidence supports

### Local marine safety context

A Pattaya Mail report dated 23 May 2026 describes a multi-agency meeting held on 21 May at the Marine Office at Laem Bali Hai Pier. It reports measures discussed or agreed for local marine tourism, including checks of vessel licences/registration and captain qualifications, alcohol/drug testing, GPS monitoring, passenger safety briefings before departures, life-saving equipment, and visible emergency contacts.

Source: [Pattaya orders zero-compromise safety push on tourist boats after high-level talks](https://www.pattayamail.com/news/pattaya-orders-zero-compromise-safety-push-on-tourist-boats-after-high-level-talks-550422) (Pattaya Mail, 23 May 2026).

**Important limitation:** this is a local news report about safety measures. It does not establish that operators are legally required to create or retain a particular digital departure record, that inspectors demand such a record, or that operators currently struggle to prove compliance. Those questions require primary-source legal/regulatory review and direct evidence.

### Existing software is a serious alternative

Bókun markets tour-operator software with booking management, channel distribution, and resource allocation. Its published START plan is listed at $49/month plus a 1.5% online booking fee; PLUS and PREMIUM are listed at $149/month and $499/month with lower stated booking fees. Bókun also describes a limited free plan for managing Viator and offline bookings.

Sources:
- [Bókun pricing and plan overview](https://www.bokun.io/grow/switch-to-bokun)
- [Bókun resource management](https://www.bokun.io/resource-management)
- [Bókun's free tour booking software overview](https://www.bokun.io/free-tour-booking-software)

These are vendor-published descriptions and prices, not an independent assessment of fit for a particular Thai operator. They nevertheless weaken any undifferentiated claim that small operators lack affordable booking/resource-management software.

## Narrow hypothesis worth falsifying

**Hypothesis A1:** Some small marine operators have a recurring and consequential gap between the safety/coordination checks they actually perform before a departure and their ability to reliably show who checked what, when, and for which vessel/trip.

Possible consequences to test—not assume—include repeated manual work, missed checks, uncertainty during handover, delayed departures, or difficulty responding to an inspection or incident.

This is deliberately narrower than a booking platform or generic operations SaaS. It is not yet a product recommendation.

## Critical unknowns

- What is the real workflow for the last few departures?
- Which checks are mandatory under applicable primary law or official instructions, and which are local operational practices?
- What evidence, if any, must be retained or shown, to whom, and for how long?
- What records are already maintained (paper, chat, photos, spreadsheets, booking system, vessel documents)?
- Has a recent inspection, incident, near miss, or handover exposed a costly gap?
- Who owns the process and who would pay to improve it?
- Would an existing booking platform, checklist, shared folder, or simple paper process solve the issue adequately?
- What legal, insurance, liability, data-protection, and operational requirements would apply to a proposed service?

## Cheapest falsification step (not authorized or performed)

If the founder later authorizes it, conduct one consented workflow observation and record review with one marine operator. Start with the most recent completed departure, not a product demo or a hypothetical feature wishlist.

Ask the operator to walk through:
1. What actually happened before that departure.
2. Which checks and documents were used.
3. Where each item was recorded or stored.
4. What was delayed, repeated, missed, or difficult to retrieve.
5. Whether this caused a real cost, risk, or operational consequence recently.

Do not ask for sensitive passenger records or copy documents without explicit consent and a clear data-handling plan. Do not contact anyone or begin a pilot until the founder explicitly authorizes it and legal questions have been scoped.

## Stop conditions

Stop or materially revise this hypothesis if:
- current records and checks are already reliable and low-friction;
- no recent consequential failure or repeated burden can be identified;
- the relevant proof is not required and has no demonstrated operational value;
- existing tools solve the problem adequately;
- no identifiable buyer owns the pain or has a plausible budget;
- legal / liability exposure makes a small founder-led solution impractical.

## Assessment

This is the **cheapest candidate for a focused falsification step**, not the most promising business opportunity and not a validated niche. The evidence currently supports only the existence of a local marine safety context and the availability of incumbent software.
