# Business and Validation Operating Model

**Status:** proposed founder operating model  
**Default constraints:** $0 cash spend; founder-light; no cold outreach or sales calls unless explicitly reconsidered.

## 1. Business model is part of product discovery

For every opportunity, identify:

- user: who uses the product?
- buyer: who pays?
- supplier / partner: who provides the underlying inventory or service?
- beneficiary: who receives the economic value?
- transaction: what event creates value?
- monetization: subscription, transaction fee, lead fee, licensing, service fee, or another model?
- cost to serve: onboarding, support, verification, refunds, payment fees, operations, and acquisition.

Do not choose a monetization model only because competitors use it.

## 2. Marketplace-specific checks

For any marketplace or aggregator hypothesis, validate both sides independently.

**Demand side**
- Is search / comparison materially difficult today?
- What does the customer compare: price, availability, trust, location, terms, language, convenience?
- What triggers the search and how often does it happen?
- Will the customer complete the transaction through a new intermediary?

**Supply side**
- Does the supplier want incremental demand?
- Can the supplier provide accurate price and availability?
- Who confirms, fulfils, cancels, refunds, and supports the transaction?
- What commission or fee is commercially acceptable?
- Is supply sufficiently differentiated and reliable?

A catalogue without reliable supply data is not a functioning aggregator. A supplier dashboard without demand is not a business.

## 3. Concierge before software

When possible, test the proposed value manually:

- use a spreadsheet as the source of truth;
- collect requests through a simple form or existing messaging channel;
- manually match demand and supply;
- confirm details directly with the responsible provider;
- record time spent, failure points, conversion, cancellations, and gross margin.

The manual test must be honest about what is and is not automated. Do not imply live inventory, instant confirmation, or verified listings if those capabilities do not exist.

## 4. Unit economics

For each completed transaction, estimate:

Revenue:
- commission or fee actually retained;
- subscription or service revenue attributable to the customer.

Variable costs:
- payment processing;
- supplier discounts / incentives;
- refunds and chargebacks;
- customer support and manual coordination;
- verification and content operations;
- acquisition cost.

Contribution margin = revenue retained minus variable costs.

Also track:
- conversion from qualified request to paid transaction;
- cancellation / refund rate;
- time to fulfil;
- support minutes per transaction;
- repeat purchase / referral rate;
- supplier retention and data freshness.

Gross booking value is not revenue. A high booking volume can still lose money.

## 5. Founder operating constraints

The current default is:

- no paid ads or paid research;
- no cold outreach;
- no sales calls;
- no public launch or publishing without approval;
- no production customer data in the demo;
- no backend or infrastructure before evidence;
- warm introductions and organic inbound may be considered, but access is not consent or validation.

These constraints can be changed deliberately, not silently assumed away.

## 6. Stage gates

**Discovery:** understand the customer, workflow, alternatives, and pain.

**Evidence:** repeated problem evidence plus a reachable segment and a credible commercial hypothesis.

**Prototype:** one narrow workflow and one named evaluation opportunity; build only what is necessary.

**Pilot:** explicit scope, named owner, consent, support plan, data safeguards, commercial terms, and exit criteria.

**Repeatability:** repeat paid use, acceptable contribution margin, manageable onboarding/support, and retention.

Passing one gate does not automatically authorize the next.

## 7. Stop conditions

Pause or stop if:

- the incumbent workflow is good enough and users have no meaningful reason to switch;
- the problem is infrequent or low consequence;
- supply-side data cannot be kept accurate;
- customer acquisition costs exceed plausible contribution margin;
- the business depends on manual work that cannot be priced or scaled;
- legal, safety, trust, or platform-policy requirements make the model impractical;
- the founder constraints make the minimum viable go-to-market impossible.

A disciplined stop is a successful learning outcome.
