# Validation Decision Framework — TourOps

**Status:** decision aid, not an automatic gate.  
**Default when evidence is insufficient:** pause feature development and continue only if a useful, permitted discovery step exists.

## Principles

- Small qualitative samples are directional, not statistically representative.
- There is no fixed interview quota or deadline. Do not continue collecting conversations merely to reach a number.
- One concrete disconfirming case may outweigh several compliments or hypothetical positives.
- Public research can describe the market and competitors; it cannot establish private workflows or willingness to pay.
- Every material decision must cite E-IDs in the [market evidence register](./market-evidence-register.md).
- No evidence ID means no claim of validation.
- A decision to continue is limited to the specific problem and segment supported by evidence; it does not approve the backlog or full SaaS.

## Possible outcomes

### D1 — Continue discovery

Use when a concrete, potentially consequential problem appears, but important questions remain. Choose one next observation or comparison that can change the decision.

### D2 — Narrow the segment

Use when evidence suggests the problem concentrates in a particular operator type, fleet/staff band, channel, or location. State why other segments differ and what remains unknown.

### D3 — Change workflow priority

Use when the pain appears real but the demo sequence is wrong. For example, bookings may arrive pre-confirmed, while resource coordination or balance reconciliation is the actual issue.

### D4 — Rework the value proposition

Use when operators describe a different outcome or problem than the product currently assumes. Use their actual behavior and language, not a polished marketing interpretation.

### D5 — Pause

Use when access is blocked, evidence is vague or contradictory, the problem is low-consequence, or the next step would violate founder constraints. A pause is a valid outcome, not a failure.

### D6 — Abandon a hypothesis or direction

Use when independent evidence consistently contradicts a specific hypothesis, or the core problem and commercial path no longer look credible. Preserve the evidence and reasoning in the decision log.

## Prototype consideration — separate explicit decision

A narrow prototype may be proposed only when the evidence supports all relevant points:

- a specific problem has been described through concrete recent behavior, not compliments;
- the problem repeats independently or has otherwise compelling corroboration;
- frequency and consequences justify a change;
- the current workaround leaves a meaningful gap;
- the target user and likely buyer are identifiable;
- a consenting operator has explicitly offered a practical opportunity to evaluate a scoped prototype;
- the experiment has a no-upfront-cost path and does not require unapproved data, outreach, publishing, integrations, or commitments.

Even then, the result is permission to discuss a small experiment, not automatic approval to build. The founder makes a fresh decision.
