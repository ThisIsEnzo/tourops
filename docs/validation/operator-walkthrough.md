# Operator Walkthrough — Optional Discovery Guide

**Purpose:** understand one operator's actual workflow before showing TourOps.  
**Use only:** a warm, permission-based introduction the founder has explicitly chosen to pursue.  
**No cold outreach, walk-ups, unsolicited DMs, or sales pitch.**

A conversation is optional, not a quota. One conversation is a useful first learning step, not validation by itself.

## Before starting

Explain that this is exploratory research, not a sales call; participation is voluntary; notes will be anonymized; they may skip questions or stop. Ask permission before taking notes.

Do not request customer/passenger names, contact details, payment records, chat screenshots, employee personal data, or confidential revenue figures. Use ranges only if the operator volunteers them.

Use the operator's preferred language. Record translation or language limitations as a source of uncertainty.

## 1. Reconstruct a real booking

- "Could you walk me through the most recent booking, from first contact to departure?"
- "Where did it start? Who contacted whom, and through which channel?"
- "What happened next?"
- "Was the price fixed or did someone prepare a custom quote?"
- "Did the booking arrive directly, through an agent, or through a marketplace?"

Listen for the actual sequence. Do not assume enquiry → quote → booking.

## 2. Understand the current system

- "Where is the booking information recorded, if anywhere?"
- "How do you know it is confirmed?"
- "How do you check availability and assign the boat, crew, equipment, or transfer?"
- "How do you track deposits and remaining balances?"
- "What information stays in chat, a notebook, a spreadsheet, or someone's memory?"

If they offer to show a process, observe structure only. Do not copy private data.

## 3. Explore a concrete problem

- "Can you remember the last time something went wrong or nearly went wrong?"
- "What exactly happened, roughly when?"
- "How often does something like that happen?"
- "What was the consequence — time, cost, lost booking, refund, or customer issue?"
- "What did you do to resolve it?"
- "Have you changed anything since then?"

If no recent incident comes to mind, record that honestly. Do not manufacture a problem by suggesting one.

## 4. Adoption and alternatives

- "Have you tried another tool or changed this process before? What happened?"
- "Who would have to enter or maintain the information every day?"
- "What would still happen outside a new tool?"
- "What makes the current approach work well?"
- "What makes it frustrating, if anything?"

Only discuss current spend if the operator raises it naturally. Do not pitch a price or ask hypothetical willingness-to-pay questions.

## 5. Optional demo probe

Show the synthetic demo only after understanding the workflow and only if the participant agrees.

Say clearly that all data is fictional and the demo is not a live booking system. Ask:
- "Which steps match what you actually do?"
- "Which steps would you skip?"
- "Where would this create duplicate work?"
- "What important part of your real process is missing?"

Observe hesitation, misread labels, language friction, and who would actually use the system. Do not defend the design or ask whether they like it.

## After the conversation

Use [the feedback template](./operator-feedback-template.md). Separate observed behavior, reported incidents, opinions, and speculation. Add only supported claims to the evidence register. Update the strategy scorecard only when its definitions are met.

The next action is decided after reviewing this conversation; do not automatically schedule another one.
