## 2. Why this network matters

The access paths expose TourOps to several adjacent operating environments:
- accommodation / multi-property hospitality;
- restaurant operations;
- mixed hospitality and activity venues;
- excursion and activity coordination;
- the existing initial research wedge: independent marine-experience operators.

This is useful for discovering whether a recurring operational problem exists across business types. It does **not** establish that one generic platform should serve all of them.

The 22-hotel network may offer the largest potential distribution opportunity, while the multi-activity farm may be especially useful for observing cross-service coordination. Hops Brewhouse may provide a narrower restaurant workflow. The excursion organizer may offer domain knowledge even if there is no commercial opportunity.

## 3. Recommended discovery sequence

1. **Farm / multi-activity venue:** explore how daily work moves between the hotel, sauna, horse riding, ATV activities and bar. Look for recurring handoffs, task ownership, scheduling conflicts and maintenance follow-up.
2. **Hotel network owner or an appropriate operations manager:** ask which operational issue repeats across properties and whether the same process is handled differently at each hotel. If possible, identify one property and one workflow for deeper discovery.
3. **Hops Brewhouse:** explore one practical restaurant workflow, such as shift handover, opening/closing checks or equipment/maintenance follow-up.
4. **Excursion organizer:** use a short, low-effort learning conversation if naturally available. Do not invest in custom development or treat the contact as a prospective paying customer without evidence.

This is a suggested order for learning, not a claim that any contact has agreed to participate. The founder may choose the easiest, most natural introduction first.

## 4. Questions to ask

Start with the person's actual work rather than presenting a product pitch:
- What recurring operational task causes the most friction?
- Can you walk through the last time it happened?
- How often does it happen, and what does it cost in time, money, rework or customer experience?
- Who notices the problem and who is responsible for fixing it?
- What tools or workarounds are used today?
- Is this a problem at one location or across multiple locations?
- Have you already tried to solve it? What happened?
- Would fixing it be important enough to allocate budget to, if a practical solution existed?

Ask permission before taking notes or observing workflows. Do not request customer, guest, employee or payment data. Use anonymized process descriptions.

## 5. Product strategy guardrails

- Keep the current marine-operator thesis as the **primary documented hypothesis** until evidence supports changing it.
- Treat hotels, restaurants, multi-activity venues and excursion operators as **adjacent discovery segments**, not approved product verticals.
- Do not assume that task management, checklists, maintenance tickets or scheduling are valuable simply because they sound common. Validate frequency, impact, current alternatives and buyer priority.
- Look for the same narrow workflow independently appearing in multiple businesses. Similar vocabulary is not enough; compare the actual trigger, steps, users, data and desired outcome.
- If different segments have materially different problems, choose one ICP for the first product instead of forcing a broad platform.
- Do not build a bespoke free solution for any contact.
- Do not promise integrations, automation, operational savings or readiness for live deployment before they exist and are validated.
- A friendly introduction, positive reaction or willingness to chat is not willingness to pay. A paid pilot or equivalent commercial commitment is the stronger test.

## 6. Founder participation and zero-budget constraints

This map is compatible with the current zero-cash validation approach, but personal introductions still require the founder to make or authorize the introduction. ChatGPT and automation can prepare research, interview guides, notes templates, comparisons and prototype materials; they cannot independently verify private operations or claim that an interview took place.

The founder prefers a technical role and does not want cold outreach or pushy selling. Keep conversations informal and permission-based. If commercial validation later requires active sales, make that a separate explicit decision; do not silently expand the founder's role.

## 7. Evidence tracking

For each contact, update the validation scorecard only after the relevant event actually occurs. Track separately:
- introduction made;
- response received;
- discovery conversation completed;
- specific recurring problem evidenced;
- impact/frequency understood;
- decision maker identified;
- prototype requested;
- pilot discussed;
- paid pilot or other commercial commitment secured.

Until then, keep the status as **access path only / unvalidated**.

## 8. Decision gate

Do not broaden TourOps into a general hospitality platform based on this network alone.

Revisit the ICP and roadmap only when:
1. multiple independent operators describe substantially the same workflow problem;
2. the problem is frequent and consequential;
3. current tools/workarounds leave a meaningful gap;
4. a buyer or budget owner confirms priority; and
5. there is a credible path to a paid pilot without violating the founder's explicit budget and operating constraints.
