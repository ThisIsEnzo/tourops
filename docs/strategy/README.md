# TourOps — Commercial Strategy

**Status:** hypothesis / zero-budget validation. Not a validated product plan.  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-18  
**Operating model:** technical founder; no cold sales or paid research. Warm introductions may be used for low-pressure discovery, without assuming commercial commitment.  
**Working market:** Pattaya–Sattahip–Rayong, Thailand.

## Business objective

Find a real, repeated customer problem and establish a sustainable, profitable business with minimal financial risk. The $100k+/month net-profit ambition is a distant growth aspiration only; it must not drive near-term scope, spending, or risk.

## Current product thesis

The primary hypothesis remains focused on independent marine-experience operators: test whether they have a costly workflow gap between receiving an enquiry and delivering a confirmed trip, including quote handling, resource coordination (vessels, crew, equipment, transfers), deposit visibility and departure preparation.

Do not assume this is a real market gap. Do not begin as a generic booking engine, CRM, channel manager, AI chatbot or all-in-one hospitality suite.

Warm access to a hotel group, a multi-activity hospitality venue, a restaurant and an excursions organizer creates useful adjacent discovery opportunities. These contacts should help compare operational patterns and test whether a recurring problem exists beyond marine operators; they do **not** automatically change the ICP, product thesis or MVP scope. Any expansion must be supported by evidence from multiple independent businesses.

## Founder operating model

The founder remains focused on technical architecture and product decisions. The current $0 validation phase excludes paid researchers, contractors, agencies, advertising, subscriptions bought solely for research, cold outreach and founder-led sales calls.

Where a warm personal introduction is available, a short, low-pressure conversation about the current workflow is allowed and consistent with the $0 constraint. It is discovery, not a sales pitch. Do not pressure friends or contacts, promise custom development, or provide unpaid bespoke implementation.

Use free desk research, a realistic clickable demo with synthetic data, and an organic inbound concept page / self-serve feedback route. Prepare assets and drafts; do not claim that anything has been published or that prospects have been contacted unless confirmed.

A lack of inbound traffic is not proof that the problem does not exist. A request for information is not proof of willingness to pay. A conversation or positive reaction is not proof of willingness to pay. Only a paid pilot or equivalent commercial commitment confirms willingness to pay.

## Current decision

**Do not build the full SaaS yet.** First prepare free market evidence and a demo that illustrates one workflow. Seek organic, self-selected interest and use warm introductions where natural. If genuine interest appears, qualify it without pressure. If commercial validation eventually requires broader human sales, defer that step until a revenue-share partner or another no-upfront-cost path is justified.

## Repository conventions

- Separate facts, hypotheses, decisions and unknowns.
- Date research and link sources.
- Never label a company a lead, customer, design partner or pilot until the relevant action happened.
- Never count website visits, likes or friendly encouragement as willingness to pay.
- Update the validation scorecard only with verified activity.
