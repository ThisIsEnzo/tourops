# ICP & Customer Discovery

**Status:** provisional ICP. Validate before committing product scope.  
**Review date:** 2026-11-16

## 1. Initial ICP hypothesis

### Primary segment to test

Independent marine-experience operators around Pattaya, Na Jomtien/Ocean Marina, Sattahip and Rayong that:
- sell private charters, fishing, sailing, diving/snorkelling or other sea-based experiences;
- handle a mix of fixed departures and quote-led/custom bookings;
- coordinate at least two constrained resource types (e.g. vessels + crew, boats + instructors, capacity + equipment);
- receive enquiries from multiple channels (website, LINE, WhatsApp, phone, agents/OTAs);
- have an owner, sales manager or operations manager accessible to make a buying decision;
- experience enough booking frequency or booking value that coordination errors and manual work matter.

### Best-fit characteristics to validate

| Dimension | Initial hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Company size | Roughly 3–30 operational staff; may use contractors |
| Fleet/resources | 2+ vessels or a meaningful network of partner vessels/crew |
| Booking pattern | Private quotes plus scheduled trips, add-ons or multi-day courses |
| Sales channels | Website + messaging + referrals/agents; actual mix unknown |
| Current stack | Spreadsheet, shared calendar, chat, booking software or a combination |
| Buyer | Owner/GM, head of sales, operations manager |
| Trigger | Double-booking, missed follow-up, quote delays, departure-day confusion, growth in fleet/partners |
| Ability to pay | Must be tied to measurable value; not assumed from business size |

These are qualification hypotheses, not hard eligibility requirements. Interview smaller and larger operators to learn where pain and buying authority concentrate.

## 2. Buyer and user roles

- **Economic buyer:** owner, managing director or general manager.
- **Operational champion:** operations manager, charter coordinator or dive centre manager.
- **Daily users:** reservations/sales staff, dispatcher, crew lead, instructors.
- **Influencers:** accountant, marina/partner operator, agency or channel manager.

Interview the person who experiences the work and the person who controls budget. They may be different people.

## 3. Disqualifiers / weak-fit signals

- One simple product, one resource, low booking frequency and no meaningful coordination.
- Most bookings are fully handled by an existing platform and the team is satisfied.
- The owner cannot identify a costly recurring problem.
- The buyer only wants a free website or one-off custom feature.
- No willingness to pay for a pilot after seeing the proposed outcome.
- The problem occurs rarely and has low financial or operational impact.

Do not disqualify automatically based on staff count, technology maturity or language.

## 4. Discovery interview guide (30–40 minutes)

Do not pitch first. Ask for recent examples and observe the actual workflow.

### Context
1. Walk me through your business and the experiences you sell.
2. How many trips/bookings did you handle last week and last month?
3. What types of bookings are fixed-price versus custom quote?
4. Who handles enquiries, confirms trips and prepares departure?

### Recent booking walkthrough
5. Take the last booking from first enquiry to completed trip. What happened at each step?
6. Where did the information live (LINE, WhatsApp, email, spreadsheet, calendar, booking platform)?
7. Who checked vessel, crew, guide, equipment and transfer availability?
8. How was the quote created and approved?
9. How did you record deposit, remaining balance and cancellation terms?
10. What did the team need on departure day?

### Pain and economics
11. What went wrong most recently? Please describe the last specific incident.
12. How often does that happen?
13. What did it cost in staff time, refund, lost revenue, reputation or owner intervention?
14. How many enquiries fail to receive a follow-up? How do you know?
15. Which task do you personally wish would disappear?
16. What have you already tried? Why did it not solve the problem?

### Existing tools and buying
17. Which software do you pay for today, and what does it cost?
18. What do you like and dislike about it?
19. If you could fix one workflow in the next 30 days, which would you choose?
20. Who approves a purchase and what budget/process is involved?
21. Would you pay for a short pilot that solves this specific workflow? What would make it worth paying for?

### Close
22. Can we observe the current process using dummy or anonymized data?
23. Who else should we speak with?
24. May we return with a concrete pilot proposal?

Avoid leading questions such as "Would you use an all-in-one platform?" and avoid treating hypothetical enthusiasm as validation.

## 5. Evidence to capture after every interview

- Company and respondent role.
- Business type, fleet/resources, booking volume (ranges acceptable).
- Booking sources and current systems.
- Exact recent workflow and specific pain incident.
- Frequency and measurable impact.
- Existing software spend and alternatives.
- Decision maker and buying timeline.
- Problem score and confidence.
- Whether a paid pilot was offered, accepted, declined or deferred.
- Next action and date.

Never record customer personal data beyond what is necessary and authorized. Use anonymized notes for product discovery.

## 6. Qualification score (0–2 each)

| Signal | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain frequency | Rare/unknown | Monthly | Weekly/daily |
| Financial/operational impact | No impact shown | Indirect/time cost | Quantified lost money/risk |
| Current workaround | Satisfactory | Some friction | Multiple tools/manual re-entry |
| Buying authority | Unknown/no access | Influencer | Decision maker engaged |
| Willingness to pay | No | Conditional | Paid pilot accepted |

Interpretation: 8–10 = prioritize; 5–7 = continue discovery; 0–4 = low priority. This is an internal prioritization aid, not a validated predictive score.

## 7. Validation threshold

Before committing to a SaaS MVP, aim for:
- 15–20 interviews in the same broad segment;
- at least 5 operators independently describing substantially the same costly workflow;
- at least 3 qualified buyers willing to discuss a paid pilot;
- at least 2 paid pilots or equivalent deposits;
- repeated use and measurable value in live operations.

If different customer types describe different problems, split the ICP rather than forcing a generic product.
