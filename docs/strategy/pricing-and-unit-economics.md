# Pricing & Unit Economics

**Status:** future price hypotheses only; not published rates or validated willingness to pay.  
**Currency:** THB.  
**Review date:** 2026-11-16  
**Current validation budget:** $0.

## 1. Current rule

Do not spend money to test price during the first zero-budget stage. Do not publish a fixed price until the workflow and buyer are clearer. Use the ranges below only as hypotheses for a later paid-pilot conversation or structured pricing test.

## 2. Initial price hypotheses

| Offer | Indicative hypothesis | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Workflow setup | ฿5,000–฿12,000 one-time | Only if setup produces a concrete operational outcome |
| Founding pilot | ฿3,000–฿6,000 / month for 6–8 weeks | One location/team and one agreed workflow |
| Starter | ฿2,500–฿4,000 / month | Limited team/resources |
| Operations | ฿5,000–฿9,000 / month | More resource planning and operational reporting |
| Multi-operation | ฿10,000–฿18,000+ / month | Multiple teams/locations or complex partner coordination |

These ranges are placeholders for learning, not a price list, competitor-parity claim or evidence that customers will pay. The first pilot may instead be a fixed-fee project if that is easier for the buyer to approve.

## 3. How to validate pricing later

Tie the offer to one measured problem. Ask what the current process costs in staff time, errors and missed revenue. Compare the proposed fee with the customer's own quantified value. Test two price points across comparable qualified buyers. Record the exact objection: price, urgency, authority, trust, functionality or timing. A paid pilot or deposit is stronger evidence than a positive reaction.

## 4. Unit economics to measure

Per customer: setup/onboarding hours × internal hourly cost; hosting and third-party API cost; support/account-management hours; messaging/payment fees if any; subscription and services revenue; refunds/credits and collection costs; gross and contribution margin; payback time for onboarding.

Monthly contribution:

Contribution = recurring revenue - variable infrastructure - messaging/API fees - support labor - other direct delivery costs.

Count founder time even if it is not paid as salary. Zero cash expenditure does not mean zero economic cost.

## 5. Illustrative sensitivity only

At ฿5,000 MRR, assume ฿500 direct software cost and two support hours at ฿300/hour. Contribution before fixed overhead and tax would be ฿3,900.

At ฿9,000 MRR, assume ฿900 direct software cost and three support hours at ฿300/hour. Contribution before fixed overhead and tax would be ฿7,200.

All inputs are illustrative. Replace them with actual costs before making a pricing decision.

## 6. Financial guardrails

No new paid subscriptions, ads, paid APIs or contractors in the current validation phase. No custom integration without funded scope. Collect a setup fee or pilot payment before substantial customer-specific implementation. Do not hold customer funds or store raw card details. Get qualified Thai accounting advice on invoicing, VAT and tax before commercial operations scale.
