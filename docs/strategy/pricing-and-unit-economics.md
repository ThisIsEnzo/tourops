# Pricing & Unit Economics

**Status:** price hypotheses for willingness-to-pay tests, not published rates.  
**Currency:** THB.  
**Review date:** 2026-11-16

## 1. Pricing principle

Price against measurable value and operational complexity, not feature count or the cheapest competitor. Keep the first offer easy to understand and low-risk for both sides.

Do not promise savings or revenue lift until measured with a customer.

## 2. Initial price tests

| Offer | Hypothesis to test | What is included |
|---|---:|---|
| Paid workflow audit / setup | ฿5,000–฿12,000 one-time | Map current process, configure booking/resource register, templates and staff walkthrough |
| Founding pilot | ฿3,000–฿6,000 per month for 6–8 weeks | One location/team, one agreed workflow, onboarding and weekly review |
| Starter | ฿2,500–฿4,000 / month | Small operator, limited staff/resources, core booking and run-sheet workflow |
| Operations | ฿5,000–฿9,000 / month | More resources/users, resource planning, payment status and reporting |
| Multi-operation | ฿10,000–฿18,000+ / month | Multiple teams/locations or complex partner/resource coordination; quote after discovery |

These are experimental ranges, not a final price list or claim about Thai market willingness to pay. Test the price alongside a concrete outcome. Do not discount indefinitely; founding pricing should have an end date and explicit feedback commitment.

## 3. Alternative commercial models

- Setup fee + subscription: likely easiest to support early onboarding.
- Fixed-price pilot: useful for proving willingness to pay before a full subscription.
- Per-location or per-resource tier: test only if customer value scales with locations/resources.
- Usage or booking fee: avoid initially unless customers prefer it and the billing basis is transparent.
- Productized service: fallback if customers pay for operational improvement but workflows vary too much for SaaS.

Avoid a percentage of booking value until there is a clear reason; it may create reconciliation, refund and trust issues.

## 4. Value-based pricing conversation

Ask the customer to estimate:
- hours per week spent coordinating enquiries, resources and departure details;
- cost of the staff time involved;
- number and cost of booking errors / double bookings / missed follow-ups;
- lost or delayed bookings due to slow quotes;
- owner/manager time spent resolving exceptions.

Illustrative calculation only:

If the system saves 8 staff hours per week and the fully loaded cost is ฿250/hour, the direct time value is about ฿8,000/month (8 × 4 × 250). This is not a market fact; replace it with the customer's own data. A recurring fee must leave the customer with a clear net benefit.

## 5. Unit economics to measure

Per customer:
- setup/onboarding hours × internal hourly cost;
- monthly hosting and third-party API cost;
- support and account-management hours;
- payment or messaging fees (if any);
- subscription and services revenue;
- refunds/credits and collection costs;
- gross margin and contribution margin;
- time to recover onboarding cost.

Simple monthly contribution estimate:

Contribution = recurring revenue - variable infrastructure - messaging/API fees - support labor - other direct delivery costs.

Keep founder labor visible even if it is not initially paid as salary.

## 6. Example sensitivity (not forecast)

At ฿5,000 MRR:
- ฿500 variable software/services cost;
- 2 support hours at ฿300/hour = ฿600;
- estimated contribution before fixed overhead and tax = ฿3,900.

At ฿9,000 MRR:
- ฿900 variable software/services cost;
- 3 support hours at ฿300/hour = ฿900;
- estimated contribution before fixed overhead and tax = ฿7,200.

The inputs are illustrative. Actual payment fees, taxes, salaries, hosting, customer acquisition and churn must be measured.

## 7. Sales pricing experiments

For the first 10 qualified conversations:
- present one concrete paid pilot, not a feature menu;
- test two price points across comparable prospects;
- record objections verbatim;
- distinguish "too expensive" from "not valuable / not urgent / no authority";
- ask for a deposit or signed pilot agreement to validate intent;
- avoid free pilots unless a strategically valuable exception has a written scope and end date.

## 8. Financial discipline

- No major fixed cost before recurring demand.
- No custom integration without paid scope.
- Collect setup fee or pilot deposit before doing substantial implementation.
- Keep customer funds in the customer's/provider's payment flow; TourOps only records status until legal/payment-provider requirements are assessed.
- Model taxes and VAT with a qualified Thai accountant before commercial invoicing at scale.
